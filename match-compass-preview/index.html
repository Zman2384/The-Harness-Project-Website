Lesson M · Compass node · Green Track default

My Medical / Health Choices — Health Armor

Objective: Own your health baseline before pressure arrives

50 minGrades 6–8TEKS: 19 TAC 19 TAC 115.26(b)(1), 115.27(b)(2) — personal health and wellness

Green Track focus

Are your check-ups current? Which vaccines have you had, such as HPV and Hep B, and which others (flu, COVID, meningococcal) should you ask about? Who is your health team? What questions do you have for a doctor?

Materials

  • Vaccine Tracker handout
  • My Health Team card
  • My Health Promise

Facilitator script starter

Open: 'Your body is your armor. Before we talk about pressure from outside, we check the inside — what keeps you healthy every day?' Facilitate inventory without sharing private medical info. Normalize asking questions.

Pivot question: Which letter is still available? M is about what you carry with you — your health team.

50-minute run order

MinBlockWhat happens
5Open + Compass CheckWelcome, ground rules, compass check
10Activities 1–2Health Armor Inventory; My Health Team Map
15Activity 3 (core)Vaccine Tracker
10Activity 4Question List for Doctor
10Activity 5 + CloseMy Health Promise — close the lesson

5 activities

  1. CHECKLIST

    Health Armor Inventory

    Personal checklist — no sharing required

    Check: Sleep 8-9h? Balanced food? Movement 60m? Check-ups current? Someone to talk to?

  2. MAP

    My Health Team Map

    Name 3-5 trusted adults/clinics, fill contact role

    Adult 1: ___ Role: ___ | Adult 2: ___ | Clinic: ___

  3. TRACKER

    Vaccine Tracker

    List what you've had, circle what to ask about next visit

    HPV · Hep B · others (flu, COVID, meningococcal) — circle what to ask your doctor about at the next visit.

  4. WRITE

    Question List for Doctor

    Write 3 questions no matter how small/weird

    1. ___ 2. ___ 3. ___ — All questions are good questions.

  5. PROMISE

    My Health Promise

    Reproducible take-home

    I promise to respect my body by... Choosing my pace because I'm worth it.

Facilitator layer · Lesson M

Big idea: Protecting your health is the most powerful thing you can do for yourself.

Setup

  • Print the Health Armor Inventory, Health Team Map, Vaccine Tracker, Doctor Question List, and Health Promise.
  • Set out the M task cards (5) per group.
  • Have the CDC teen vaccine page ready to show if asked: cdc.gov/vaccines.

50-minute lesson plan

  1. 0:00 · 5 min Open: “Your body is your armor. Before we talk about pressure from outside, we check the inside.” Ground rules. Compass check: point to M.
  2. 0:05 · 10 min Health Armor Inventory, then Health Team Map. Private checklists — nothing is collected.
  3. 0:15 · 15 min Core: Vaccine Tracker + Fact-or-Myth round.
  4. 0:30 · 10 min Question List for the Doctor — every student writes three.
  5. 0:40 · 10 min My Health Promise, then the exit ticket.

Task cards + facilitator cards

Activity 1 · CHECKLIST · Solo · 5 min

Health Armor Inventory

Task card

  1. Rate each item 0 (not yet), 1 (sometimes), or 2 (most days): sleep 8–9 hours · balanced food · 60 minutes of movement · check-ups current · someone to talk to.
  2. Circle the one item you will strengthen this week.
  3. Keep this page. Nobody collects it.

Facilitator card

Say this: “This is a dashboard, not a report card. A 0 is just data.”

Watch for: Students comparing scores. Redirect: “Your armor, your numbers.”

If–then: If a student says they have no one to talk to: note it privately and connect them with the counselor after class — not in front of the group.

Debrief: Which item is easiest to strengthen first? Why?

Activity 2 · MAP · Solo + pairs · 5 min

My Health Team Map

Task card

  1. Write 3–5 adults or places on your health team: a family adult, a school adult, a clinic or doctor.
  2. Next to each, write how you reach them (in person, text, call).
  3. With a partner, practice the ask: “Can you be on my health team if I have a question?”

Facilitator card

Say this: “A team is people who answer. Pick people who actually pick up.”

Watch for: Students listing only peers. Peers can be support, but the map needs at least one adult.

If–then: If a student cannot name an adult at home: school nurse, counselor, and coach all count.

Debrief: Who on your map would you go to first? What makes them a good pick?

Activity 3 · TRACKER · CORE · Solo + groups of 3 · 15 min

Vaccine Tracker + Fact or Myth

Task card

  1. On the tracker, check what you know you have had. “Don’t know” is a fine answer — circle it.
  2. CAPE names two: HPV and Hepatitis B. Circle any others (flu, COVID, meningococcal) to ask about.
  3. In your group, sort the 4 Fact-or-Myth cards. Be ready to explain one.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Knowing what you don’t know is armor too. The next move is a question, not a guess.”

Watch for: Students sharing private medical info aloud. Remind: the tracker stays private; the card sort is the group part.

If–then: If a student raises vaccine doubts from home: “Good question for your doctor and your family. Write it on your question list.” No debate.

Debrief: What is one thing you will check on with a parent or doctor?

Card set + answer key
  • “Vaccines are only for little kids.” → MYTH — HPV vaccine is recommended at ages 11–12 (can start at 9).
  • “Hepatitis B vaccine is usually given as a baby, and teens who missed it can catch up.” → FACT
  • “If I feel fine, I don’t need a check-up.” → MYTH — a yearly check-up sets your baseline.
  • “You can ask your doctor which vaccines are due.” → FACT — questions are always allowed.

Activity 4 · WRITE · Solo · 10 min

Question List for the Doctor

Task card

  1. Write three questions for your next check-up — no question is too small or too weird.
  2. Star the one you most want answered.
  3. Practice saying it out loud once to your partner, word for word.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Doctors hear every question. The only bad question is the one you keep in your head.”

Watch for: Blank pages. Offer starters: “Is it normal that…” · “How much sleep…” · “What vaccines…”

If–then: If a question needs a private answer, it goes in the parking lot or to the nurse — not the group.

Debrief: How did it feel to say your question out loud?

Activity 5 · PROMISE · Solo · 5 min

My Health Promise

Task card

  1. Finish the sentence: “I promise to respect my body by…” with one specific action.
  2. Sign and date it. Take it home.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Choosing your pace is a promise you make because you’re worth it.”

Watch for: Vague promises (“be healthy”). Push for one action they can do this week.

If–then: If a student does not want to sign: fine. Ask them to keep it anyway.

Debrief: Read your promise to yourself. Is it specific enough to check off by Friday?

Check for understanding · exit ticket

  1. Which two vaccines does CAPE name in Health Armor?
    Answer key

    HPV and Hepatitis B.

  2. True or false: “Don’t know” is a wrong answer on the Vaccine Tracker.
    Answer key

    False. Not knowing is data. The next move is asking.

  3. Name one person on your Health Team and how you reach them.
    Answer key

    Any trusted adult or clinic plus a real way to contact them.

Success looks like: Every student leaves with a completed Health Team Map, three written doctor questions, and a signed promise.

Differentiation note

SupportPre-filled checklist with icons; sentence starters for the doctor questions; let students dictate to the co-facilitator.
ExtendLook up one vaccine on the CDC teen page and write a two-sentence summary in your own words.
Language learnersPicture cards for sleep, food, movement, and check-ups; allow questions in the home language.
Access / IEPOral responses accepted; large-print handouts; extra time on the tracker.
Trauma-informedNever require sharing health information. The tracker and inventory are never collected.

If a student discloses harm: pause calmly, move the group to the buffer activity, talk privately, and follow the Stop-the-Room script in the Kit & safety tab of the facilitator dashboard below.

Lesson A · Compass node · Green Track default

Awareness — Built-In Radar

Objective: Read your surroundings and your internal radar in real time

50 minGrades 6–8TEKS: 19 TAC 115.26(b)(3), 115.27(b)(3) — emotional health, decision-making

Green Track focus

Can you tell when something feels off? Are you on autopilot or tuned in? Tuning your built-in radar to notice shifts.

Materials

  • Gut Check Scale card
  • Body Signals diagram
  • Awareness Audit sheet

Facilitator script starter

Open: 'Everyone has a radar. Sometimes we ignore it to fit in. Let's practice noticing — data, not paranoia.' Use non-scenario examples: crowded hallway, group chat.

Pivot question: Which letter is still available? A is awareness — what does your gut say right now?

50-minute run order

MinBlockWhat happens
5Open + Compass CheckWelcome, ground rules, compass check
10Activities 1–2Gut Check Scale; Reading the Room
15Activity 3 (core)Autopilot vs Tuned In
10Activity 4Body Signals
10Activity 5 + CloseAwareness Audit — close the lesson

5 activities

  1. SCALE

    Gut Check Scale

    0-10 how safe does this feel? Data not paranoia

    0 = Not sure, 10 = Completely safe. Where are you right now? What tells you that?

  2. CUES

    Reading the Room

    3 cues to scan

    1. Body language shift 2. Exit / options 3. Energy change — Practice naming them.

  3. JOURNAL

    Autopilot vs Tuned In

    Journal prompt

    When was I performing to fit in vs listening to what I actually felt?

  4. BODY

    Body Signals

    Where do you feel discomfort?

    Chest tight? Stomach flip? Shoulders up? Jaw clenched? Map it.

  5. AUDIT

    Awareness Audit

    Create a personal pause word

    My pause word: ____ — When I feel off, I'll say/think this word first.

Facilitator layer · Lesson A

Big idea: A named feeling is data, not drama.

Setup

  • Print the Gut Check Scale, Reading the Room cue card, Body Signals map, and Awareness Audit.
  • Pick three neutral examples (crowded hallway, group chat going quiet, a game that gets too rough).

50-minute lesson plan

  1. 0:00 · 5 min Open: “Everyone has a radar. Sometimes we ignore it to fit in. Today we practice noticing.” Compass check: point to A.
  2. 0:05 · 10 min Gut Check Scale, then Reading the Room.
  3. 0:15 · 15 min Core: Autopilot vs. Tuned In journal + pair talk.
  4. 0:30 · 10 min Body Signals map.
  5. 0:40 · 10 min Awareness Audit and pause word, then the exit ticket.

Task cards + facilitator cards

Activity 1 · SCALE · Solo · 5 min

Gut Check Scale

Task card

  1. Read each everyday situation the facilitator names.
  2. Rate it 0 (not sure / feels off) to 10 (completely fine).
  3. Write one word for what told you that number.

Facilitator card

Say this: “There is no right number. We are practicing listening, not judging.”

Watch for: Students copying a neighbour’s number. Scales are private.

If–then: If a student rates an ordinary situation very low and looks upset, check in privately at the break.

Debrief: What clue did your body or brain notice first?

Activity 2 · CUES · Groups of 3 · 5 min

Reading the Room

Task card

  1. Learn the three cues: body language shifts · exits and options · energy changes.
  2. For each example card, name which cue you would notice first.
  3. Agree as a group on one move you could make.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Radar is about noticing early, while you still have options.”

Watch for: Groups jumping to dramatic outcomes. Keep it everyday.

If–then: If a group gets stuck: “What would you see? What would you hear?”

Debrief: Which cue is easiest for you to miss?

Activity 3 · JOURNAL · CORE · Solo + pairs · 15 min

Autopilot vs. Tuned In

Task card

  1. Write about a time you went along to fit in (autopilot) and a time you listened to what you actually felt (tuned in). No names.
  2. Underline what was different inside you each time.
  3. Share only the underlined part with a partner — or pass.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Everyone runs on autopilot sometimes. Leaders notice when they switch it back off.”

Watch for: Students writing about other people. Bring it back to “what did you notice in you?”

If–then: If a journal entry describes harm, follow the Stop-the-Room script after class. Do not read it aloud.

Debrief: What helps you switch from autopilot to tuned in?

Activity 4 · BODY MAP · Solo · 10 min

Body Signals

Task card

  1. On the outline, mark where you feel stress: chest tight? stomach flip? shoulders up? jaw clenched?
  2. Label your top signal.
  3. Write the CAPE Response Plan next to it: trust your gut → use your voice → reach out → remember the standard.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Your body often knows before your brain admits it.”

Watch for: Giggling about the body outline. Keep it matter-of-fact; it is a map, not a drawing contest.

If–then: If a student cannot find a signal, “not sure yet” is valid. Ask them to notice this week.

Debrief: What is your earliest signal?

Activity 5 · AUDIT · Solo · 5 min

Awareness Audit + Pause Word

Task card

  1. Pick a personal pause word — one word you will think or say when your radar pings.
  2. Write the first move you will make after the pause word.

Facilitator card

Say this: “A pause word buys you three seconds. Three seconds is enough to choose.”

Watch for: Joke words. Fine — if they will actually use it.

If–then: If a student chooses a word that is hurtful, ask for another privately.

Debrief: Say your pause word in your head. What comes right after it?

Check for understanding · exit ticket

  1. Name the three Reading the Room cues.
    Answer key

    Body language shifts, exits and options, energy changes.

  2. Put the CAPE Response Plan in order: reach out · trust your gut · remember the standard · use your voice.
    Answer key

    Trust your gut → use your voice → reach out → remember the standard.

  3. What is a pause word for?
    Answer key

    To stop and buy a few seconds to choose your next move when your radar pings.

Success looks like: Students name their earliest body signal, the three cues, and a pause word with a first move.

Differentiation note

SupportEmoji scale instead of 0–10; cue cards with pictures; partner reads examples aloud.
ExtendWrite a 3-step plan for one situation where radar matters (lunchroom, bus, group chat).
Language learnersBody-part vocabulary card; allow drawing instead of writing.
Access / IEPOffer a seated, eyes-open grounding instead of a body scan; oral journal option.
Trauma-informedBody work can surface memories. Keep it optional, eyes open, and never require sharing.

If a student discloses harm: pause calmly, move the group to the buffer activity, talk privately, and follow the Stop-the-Room script in the Kit & safety tab of the facilitator dashboard below.

Lesson T · Compass node · Green Track default

Tools & Trusted Support — Safety Kit

Objective: Having tools, team, plans that keep you safe before you need them

50 minGrades 6–8TEKS: 19 TAC 115.26(b)(4), 115.27(b)(5) — interpersonal skills, safety

Green Track focus

Phone charged? Ride plan? Person you'd actually call? NOT clinical tools — just everyday safety planning.

Materials

  • Safety Kit Inventory
  • Trusted Adult Ladder
  • Saturday Crisis Card Green

Facilitator script starter

Open: 'Tools are boring until you need them. We pack before the trip, not during.' Keep it practical: charger, ride, check-in person.

Pivot question: Which letter is still available? T — do you have a plan if plans change tonight?

50-minute run order

MinBlockWhat happens
5Open + Compass CheckWelcome, ground rules, compass check
10Activities 1–2Safety Kit Inventory; Trusted Adult Ladder
15Activity 3 (core)Safety Buddy System
10Activity 4Saturday Crisis Card Starter (Green)
10Activity 5 + CloseText Script Practice — close the lesson

5 activities

  1. INVENTORY

    Safety Kit Inventory

    What you actually have today

    Phone + charger? Ride plan? $ for bus? Trusted adult list? Check-in time?

  2. LADDER

    Trusted Adult Ladder

    1 primary, 2 backup, how to ask

    Primary: ___ How to ask: ___ | Backup 1: ___ | Backup 2: ___

  3. BUDDY

    Safety Buddy System

    Who knows where you are and when to check in

    Who knows where you are tonight? What time will you check in? Text: 'Made it home'

  4. CARD

    Saturday Crisis Card Starter (Green)

    Fill blank version only

    24hr clinic/ER blank, 211, 988, trusted adult — no clinical locators detailed in preview

  5. SCRIPT

    Text Script Practice

    Practice asking for backup

    "I'm heading out. Who wants to walk with me?" / "Can you be my check-in at 9?"

Facilitator layer · Lesson T

Big idea: We pack before the trip, not during it.

Setup

  • Print the Safety Kit Inventory, Trusted Adult Ladder, Safety Buddy plan, and the Green Saturday Crisis Card (blanks only).
  • Know your local 24-hour clinic or ER, 211, and 988 so you can help students fill blanks.

50-minute lesson plan

  1. 0:00 · 5 min Open: “Tools are boring until you need them.” Compass check: point to T.
  2. 0:05 · 10 min Safety Kit Inventory, then the Trusted Adult Ladder.
  3. 0:15 · 15 min Core: Safety Buddy System with a plan-falls-through twist.
  4. 0:30 · 10 min Saturday Crisis Card (Green).
  5. 0:40 · 10 min Text Script Practice, then the exit ticket.

Task cards + facilitator cards

Activity 1 · INVENTORY · Solo · 5 min

Safety Kit Inventory

Task card

  1. Check what you actually have today: phone + charger · ride plan · money for the bus · trusted-adult list · a check-in time.
  2. Star one item you will add before this weekend.

Facilitator card

Say this: “A kit you don’t carry is a wish list.”

Watch for: Students without phones feeling left out. A written list and a bus card count.

If–then: If cost is a barrier (no phone, no bus money), note it privately and connect with the counselor or 211.

Debrief: What is the cheapest item to add to your kit?

Activity 2 · LADDER · Solo · 5 min

Trusted Adult Ladder

Task card

  1. Write one primary adult and two backups.
  2. Under each, write exactly how you would ask for help.

Facilitator card

Say this: “A ladder has more than one rung for a reason.”

Watch for: The same person on every rung.

If–then: If a student names no adults, help them build the ladder from school staff.

Debrief: What would you do if rung one doesn’t answer?

Activity 3 · BUDDY · CORE · Pairs · 15 min

Safety Buddy System

Task card

  1. With a partner, plan an ordinary Saturday outing: where, who knows, check-in time, ride home.
  2. Draw a twist card (ride cancels, phone dies, plans change).
  3. Fix the plan. Ask: which letter is still available?

Facilitator card

Say this: “A good plan survives one thing going wrong.”

Watch for: Twist answers that rely on luck. Push for a real backup.

If–then: If a pair gets stuck, point to the ladder: who is rung two?

Debrief: What broke, and what still worked?

Activity 4 · CARD · Solo · 10 min

Saturday Crisis Card (Green)

Task card

  1. Fill the blanks: trusted adult + phone · backup adult + phone · your 24-hour clinic or ER · 211 · 988 · check-in time · ride plan.
  2. Fold it to fit your backpack or wallet.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Write it while you’re calm. Nobody remembers phone numbers in a panic.”

Watch for: Blank ER lines. Help with the local option you prepared.

If–then: Green Track uses blanks only — no detailed clinical locators.

Debrief: Where will this card live so you can find it fast?

Activity 5 · SCRIPT · Pairs · 5 min

Text Script Practice

Task card

  1. Practice two texts out loud: “I’m heading out. Who wants to walk with me?” and “Can you be my check-in at 9?”
  2. Write your own version in your words.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Scripts work when they sound like you.”

Watch for: Scripts that apologize. No apology needed for staying safe.

If–then: If a student says asking for help is embarrassing, model it yourself first.

Debrief: Which script would you actually send?

Check for understanding · exit ticket

  1. Name three things in a Safety Kit.
    Answer key

    Any three: charged phone, charger, ride plan, bus money, trusted-adult list, check-in time.

  2. Your ride cancels. What two moves can you make?
    Answer key

    Use the ladder (call a backup adult) and the buddy plan; any letter still available.

  3. What do 211 and 988 do?
    Answer key

    211: community resources (rides, food, local help). 988: mental health support, call or text, 24/7.

Success looks like: Every student has a completed Crisis Card with at least two adults, 211, and 988.

Differentiation note

SupportPre-printed card with 211 and 988 filled in; partner scribes; picture twist cards.
ExtendCreate a plan B and a plan C for the outing and present it in 30 seconds.
Language learnersBilingual crisis-card labels; practice scripts in the home language first.
Access / IEPLarge-format card; voice-memo version of scripts.
Trauma-informedSome students lack safe adults at home. Never assume family is the first rung.

If a student discloses harm: pause calmly, move the group to the buffer activity, talk privately, and follow the Stop-the-Room script in the Kit & safety tab of the facilitator dashboard below.

Lesson C · Compass node · Green Track default

Clear Communication & Consent — Strongest Voice

Objective: Saying boundaries out loud, hearing yes/no clearly

50 minGrades 6–8TEKS: 19 TAC 115.26(b)(5), 115.27(b)(6) — communication, boundaries

Green Track focus

FRIES for everyday situations: secrets, photos, sharing personal info, late invites — Freely given, Reversible, Informed, Enthusiastic, Specific. Plus the four-tier Boundary Menu.

Materials

  • FRIES cards
  • Boundary Menu 4-tier cards
  • Screenshot Reality sheet

Facilitator script starter

Open: 'Strong voices are clear, not loud. FRIES helps us check if a yes is really a yes — for photos, secrets, late invites, not just one topic.' Teach 4-tier menu.

Pivot question: Which letter is still available? C — what tier is this situation? Appetizer, Main, Special, Do Not Serve?

50-minute run order

MinBlockWhat happens
5Open + Compass CheckWelcome, ground rules, compass check
10Activities 1–2FRIES Decoder; Boundary Menu Sort
15Activity 3 (core)Strongest Voice Scripts
10Activity 4No / Delay Practice
10Activity 5 + CloseScreenshot Reality + 3-Second Rule — close the lesson

5 activities

  1. DECODER

    FRIES Decoder

    5 cards, match scenario to missing letter

    F=Freely given R=Reversible I=Informed E=Enthusiastic S=Specific — Which letter is missing here?

  2. SORT

    Boundary Menu Sort

    Sort 12 everyday situations into 4 tiers

    Appetizers (everyday interactions) / Main Courses (closer friendships, sharing personal things) / Specials (future dating or romantic situations) / Do Not Serve (firm limits, non-negotiable)

  3. VOICE

    Strongest Voice Scripts

    Practice out loud

    "I'm not ready for that. Let's keep it in the group for now." / "I'm heading out."

  4. DELAY

    No / Delay Practice

    Short phrases that buy time

    "Hold on." "Not now." "I need to think." — Practice tone = calm, clear.

  5. DIGITAL

    Screenshot Reality + 3-Second Rule

    Pause before posting

    Pause 3 seconds, run the Three Gates: the Legacy Check (do I trust this person with my reputation?), the Billboard Check (fine on a billboard?), the FRIES Check (freely given, informed, specific?). One “not sure” means don’t send.

Facilitator layer · Lesson C

Big idea: Your voice is your first line of protection — use it early and use it clearly.

Setup

  • Print the FRIES Decoder, Boundary Menu sort, Strongest Voice script card, and Three Gates check.
  • Cut the 12 Boundary Menu situation slips per group.

50-minute lesson plan

  1. 0:00 · 5 min Open: “Strong voices are clear, not loud.” Compass check: point to C.
  2. 0:05 · 10 min FRIES Decoder, then Boundary Menu Sort.
  3. 0:15 · 15 min Core: Strongest Voice scripts — four families, practiced out loud.
  4. 0:30 · 10 min No / Delay practice.
  5. 0:40 · 10 min Three Gates (3-Second Rule), then the exit ticket.

Task cards + facilitator cards

Activity 1 · DECODER · Groups of 3 · 5 min

FRIES Decoder

Task card

  1. FRIES = Freely given · Reversible · Informed · Enthusiastic · Specific.
  2. For each everyday scenario card, name the missing letter.
  3. Rewrite one line so the yes is real.

Facilitator card

Say this: “If one ingredient is missing, the yes is void — not ‘mostly there.’”

Watch for: Groups arguing about two letters. Good — both can be missing.

If–then: Keep scenarios everyday (secrets, photos, sharing info, late invites). Redirect anything explicit.

Debrief: Which letter goes missing most often in real life?

Card set + answer key
  • A friend posts your photo after you said “maybe.” → Enthusiastic
  • You agreed to share your location, then changed your mind and they say it’s too late. → Reversible
  • Someone says “just sign up” without telling you what it is. → Informed
  • You said yes to one group chat; they add you to five. → Specific
  • “If you were really my friend, you’d tell me.” → Freely given

Activity 2 · SORT · Groups of 3 · 5 min

Boundary Menu Sort

Task card

  1. Sort 12 everyday situations into four tiers: Appetizers (everyday interactions) · Main Courses (closer friendships, sharing personal things) · Specials (future dating or romantic situations) · Do Not Serve (firm limits).
  2. Your menu can differ from your group’s. Say why.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Everyone’s menu is different. Nobody orders for you.”

Watch for: Pressure to agree. Different menus are the point.

If–then: Sending or asking for nude or sexual photos is always Do Not Serve — and it has legal consequences for minors.

Debrief: Which situation did your group disagree about most?

Activity 3 · SCRIPTS · CORE · Threes: Speaker / Listener / Observer · 15 min

Strongest Voice Scripts

Task card

  1. Practice one line from each family out loud. Rotate roles after each.
  2. Set a boundary: “I’m not ready for that.” · Ask for clarity: “What do you mean?” · Hold the line: “I already answered.” / “Asking again won’t change my answer.” · Buy time: “I don’t decide big things at midnight.”
  3. Observer reports what they saw — no scores.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Reading silently does not build the muscle. Say it.”

Watch for: Speakers laughing or apologising. Have them repeat it once more, calmly.

If–then: If the listener pushes back hard in role-play, pause and reset: practice, not a test of wills.

Debrief: Which family is hardest for you? Why?

Activity 4 · DELAY · Pairs · 10 min

No / Delay Practice

Task card

  1. Practice short phrases that buy time: “Hold on.” · “Not now.” · “I need to think.”
  2. Then practice hearing a no: “Thanks for telling me.”

Facilitator card

Say this: “Hearing a no well is a skill, too.”

Watch for: Red-flag responses (“You’re being dramatic”). Name them as red flags.

If–then: If a student says they could never say no to a friend, practice the softest version first.

Debrief: What does a green-light response to your no sound like?

Activity 5 · DIGITAL · Solo · 5 min

Three Gates (3-Second Rule)

Task card

  1. Before you send or post, run three gates: Legacy Check (do I trust this person with my reputation?) · Billboard Check (fine on a billboard?) · FRIES Check (freely given, informed, specific?).
  2. Any “not sure” = do not send.
  3. Do the physical drill: phone locked, count one-two-three out loud.

Facilitator card

Say this: “Three gates. One ‘not sure’ stops the whole thing.”

Watch for: Students treating “not sure” as “think more.” It means stop.

Stop the Room — If a Student Discloses Harm

Use this the moment a student says, writes, or hints that someone is hurting them, that they are hurting themselves, or that they are in danger. You do not need proof. You do not need details.

1 · PAUSE — calm voice, no alarm“I want to pause us right here. Thank you for trusting us with that. It sounds important, and you deserve a private conversation with an adult who can help.”
2 · PROTECT THE ROOMCo-facilitator moves the group to the planned buffer activity (Habit Stack or a quiet journal prompt). The student is never singled out or asked to explain in front of peers.
3 · PRIVATE — listen, don’t investigateWithin sight of the second adult. Let the student talk. Do not ask leading questions, do not ask for details, do not contact the person named. Say: “You did the right thing by telling me.”
4 · BE HONEST ABOUT CONFIDENTIALITY“I can’t keep this just between us, because my job is to make sure you’re safe. I’ll only tell the people who need to know so they can help.” Never promise secrecy.
5 · REPORTTexas Family Code §261.101: report suspected abuse or neglect to DFPS (1-800-252-5400 or txabusehotline.org) or law enforcement within 48 hours of first suspecting it. You must make the report yourself — you cannot hand it off. Also follow district policy (counselor and administrator). Immediate danger: call 911 now.
6 · SELF-HARM OR SUICIDE RISKDo not leave the student alone. Walk them to the counselor. 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text) is a backup, not a replacement for the counselor.
7 · DOCUMENTWrite the student’s exact words, the time, and who you told. No interpretation, no opinions. Store it per district policy — not in this kit.
8 · CLOSE THE GROUP + CAREBack in the room: “Sometimes this work brings things up. If anything today felt heavy, you can talk to [counselor] or either of us.” Debrief with your co-facilitator afterward.

Never:

  • Never promise to keep it secret.
  • Never investigate, ask “why,” or ask for details.
  • Never confront the person named.
  • Never discuss it with other students or staff who do not need to know.
  • Never wait for the session to end if the student is in immediate danger.

A role-play line is not a disclosure. A real statement about a real person is — even if it comes during a role-play.

If a student discloses harm

Pause calmly. Move the group to the buffer activity. Talk privately, within sight of the second adult. Don’t investigate and don’t promise secrecy. Report under Tex. Fam. Code §261.101 within 48 hours (DFPS 1-800-252-5400) — immediate danger: 911. Full script in the Kit & safety tab.
