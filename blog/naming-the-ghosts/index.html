Naming the Ghosts: why fear is data, not a verdict

CZChristopher ZacharieSeptember 2, 2026 — 2 min read — The 1 a.m. Reality
The 1 a.m. Reality12:38 a.m.

Things are going well. You meant to bring up condoms, or your PrEP, or when you last tested. The words are right there.

Then a voice says if you ask, this ends. So you say nothing, and your plan skips a step you never decided to skip.

The pivotMATCH Framework

What a ghost is

A ghost is a fear that shows up at the worst moment and skips a step in your plan. Fear of rejection skips the condom conversation. Fear of cost skips the clinic visit.

The 16 ghosts we see most

Rejection, Cost, Broken Condoms, Disclosure, Judgment, Timing, Pain, Trust, Silence, Past, Future, Comparison, Shame, Access, Body, and Mood-Killer.

Turn a ghost into a task

  1. Name it. Say which ghost is loudest for you.
  2. Map it. Match it to the MATCH piece it attacks. Cost usually hits Medications. Rejection usually hits Communication.
  3. Give it a task. Cost gets "look up sliding-scale clinics." Rejection gets "write and rehearse one sentence."
"Silence is risk. Conversation is protection." — Christopher Zacharie
Key takeaway

A fear with a name and a task stops making decisions for you.

Bring it home

Unnamed fear doesn't disappear. It just decides for you. Once a ghost has a name and a task, it's no longer a ghost. It's the next step in your plan.
