Doxy-PEP: a 72-hour backup for bacterial STIs

CZChristopher ZacharieOctober 16, 2026 — 3 min read — The 1 a.m. Reality
The 1 a.m. Reality7:40 a.m.

Last night was good. This morning, you remember there was no condom. You're on PrEP, so HIV isn't what's on your mind.

What is: the syphilis diagnosis you dealt with in the spring. You don't want a repeat. Your provider mentioned something called doxy-PEP, and you never followed up.

The pivotMATCH Framework

Name the Ghosts

  • Shame: "Needing this means I'm reckless." Using every tool available is a strategy, not a confession.
  • Judgment: "My provider will think less of me." Asking for doxy-PEP is the conversation your provider is trained to have.
  • Access: "I don't have a prescription." You can ask for one in advance, so it's in your bag before you need it.
  • Timing: "It's too late now." You have up to 72 hours. Act today.

What doxy-PEP is

Doxy-PEP (doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis) is an antibiotic taken after sex to lower the chance of getting certain bacterial STIs.

How it's used: one 200 mg dose of doxycycline, taken as soon as possible and within 72 hours after oral, anal, or vaginal sex. Never more than 200 mg in 24 hours.

  • What it reduces: in large trials, syphilis and chlamydia by more than 70%. Early trials also showed partial protection against gonorrhea, but more recent data suggest that benefit has faded as resistance spreads. (Updated October 25, 2026.)
  • What it doesn't touch: HIV, mpox, herpes, or HPV. Keep PrEP, vaccines, and condoms in your plan.
  • Who it's recommended for: CDC recommends it for gay and bisexual men and transgender women who've had syphilis, chlamydia, or gonorrhea in the past 12 months. If that's not you, ask your provider whether it fits your situation.

Your next three moves

  1. Call today. Your PrEP provider, a sexual health clinic, or a telehealth service can prescribe it.
  2. Say it plainly. Use the script below.
  3. Book your testing. People using doxy-PEP should test for STIs every 3 months.
"I'm interested in doxy-PEP. I had a bacterial STI this year and I want a prescription I can keep on hand."

Map it to MATCH

  • Medications: doxy-PEP sits beside PrEP, not in place of it.
  • Awareness: test every 3 months, and know your results.
  • Tools: condoms still prevent HIV and STIs that doxy-PEP misses.
  • Communication: tell partners what you're using, and ask your provider about side effects and how to take it.
  • Habits: keep a filled prescription where you'll have it within 72 hours.

This is education, not medical advice. Talk with a healthcare provider about whether doxy-PEP is right for you.

Key takeaway

Doxy-PEP is a backup with a 72-hour clock. Have it before you need it.

Bring it home

A strong plan has layers, and every layer has a job. Doxy-PEP covers what PrEP doesn't. Testing tells you whether the plan is working. Add the layer before 1 a.m. asks for it.
